# STE-inspired writing

Read for dense-text rewrites or explicit requests about ASD-STE100.

## Relaxed clarity mode

This project's default is an original plain-language guide inspired by public STE
principles. It is not a substitute for the standard or its controlled dictionary.

- Split sentences when they mix unrelated ideas or sequential instructions.
- State who acts and what they act on. Use direct instructions for procedures.
- Keep names stable. Repeating a technical term is often clearer than a synonym.
- Explain unfamiliar terms instead of deleting useful technical information.
- State a condition before the action that depends on it.
- Preserve distinctions between permission, obligation, prediction, and possibility.
- Keep a sequence numbered when order matters. Use prose when relationships matter.
- Prefer literal wording for procedures. In teaching, use an analogy if it helps;
  identify where the analogy stops being accurate.
- Use sentence length as a readability signal, not an arbitrary compliance score.

Example, written for this project:

**Dense:** "In the event that the number of incoming tasks exceeds the processing
capacity, an accumulation of pending work may be observed."

**Clear:** "If incoming tasks exceed capacity, pending work may build up."

This keeps the original possibility instead of changing it into a certainty.
Preserve any source conditions about tasks being discarded or rerouted.

Spanish example (inspired style, not formal STE):

**Dense:** "Se deberá proceder al reinicio del equipo únicamente tras haberse
confirmado la finalización del respaldo."

**Clear:** "Confirma que el respaldo terminó. Después, reinicia el equipo."

Preserve "must" if the output is a policy or a contractual requirement. A direct
instruction is suitable here because the example is a procedure.

## Requests for formal STE

If the user needs strict ASD-STE100 drafting or assessment:

1. Identify the issue of the standard and whether the text is procedural or descriptive.
2. Use an authorized copy of the applicable rules and dictionary, and the domain glossary.
   Refer the user to the official standard if these are unavailable.
3. Check word meanings and permitted grammatical uses as well as sentence structure.
   A familiar word does not prove that its use is approved.
4. Identify the rules actually checked and items requiring review. Do not present a
   general rewrite, word count, or LLM judgment as verified full compliance.

Without the required references, offer an STE-inspired draft and clearly state its
status. Help with clarity without claiming compliance. Do not translate a
non-English text into English unless requested or necessary to a stated STE task.

## Primary sources

- [Official STE FAQ](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_faq.html): rules, dictionary,
  technical writing, and adoption of clarity principles in other contexts.
- [Official standard request and AI guidance](https://asd-ste100.org/STE_downloads.html):
  obtain the standard and distinguish plausible output from verified compliance.
- [Official guidance on tools](https://asd-ste100.org/STEsoftware.html): scope and
  limitations of software assistance.

No standard text or controlled dictionary is bundled in this project.
